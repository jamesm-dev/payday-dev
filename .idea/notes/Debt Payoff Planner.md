# Debt Payoff Planner

## Pitch

Compare payoff strategies side by side — highest interest first (avalanche) against smallest balance
first (snowball) — and see how far an extra amount per paycheck, or the two third-paycheck months
each year, moves your debt-free date.

## Why it might work

- Bi-weekly pay means 26 paychecks a year, so two months a year bring a third one. Committing those
  in advance to the most expensive loan is a debt plan that takes nothing from the normal budget —
  the awkward pay rhythm becomes the weapon.
- What-ifs make trade-offs visible: "this much less a week means debt-free this many months sooner".
- It can model a refinance or consolidation too. The biggest lever may be a lower rate, not spending
  less.

## Why it might not

- If every paycheck is already spoken for, there is no extra to plan with, and the planner is
  decoration until spending changes.
- Snowball against avalanche is partly psychological. The app can show the cost difference; it
  cannot choose for you.

> [!warning] Riskiest assumption
> That there is money left after minimum payments and bills to put toward debt at all. The first two
> pay periods will show it.

## What would raise confidence

- [ ] Once the loan list exists, check whether minimum payments plus bills fit inside two paychecks a
  month. The gap is the plan's fuel.
